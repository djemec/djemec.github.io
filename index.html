The Story

I've always wanted to understand how things actually work. Diving deep started in college where I studied genetics because I was curious about the machinery inside cells. I ended up doing research on virtual screening to better understand how simulation technology could advance our understanding of biology. Then, I got curious about something else: how does business work and what's it like to get paid in a real job? So I spent a few years doing consulting, delivering custom processes and software for pharmas, life science, supply chain, banks, and medical device companies. What I found was that I really enjoyed the part where you figure out what to build, watch people use it, and improve it. What I didn't like was cutting the engagement just when there was traction.

That led me to product management, which I merged with my fascination with biology. At DNAnexus I grew the clinico-genomic data sharing and analysis platform from a single customer. I also pitched and built a scientific product R&D team built of engineers and scientists working on productionizing research algorithms. With the software and team we made it possible for researchers to analyze huge genomic datasets without having to think about the infrastructure. It grew to be a large portion of the company's strategy and revenue and we also got to power the UK Biobank's research platform. At AWS I did similar things at larger scale: built a molecular data platform, shipped drug discovery tools with AI integration, turned around a healthcare NLP product. The interesting pattern I noticed was that compute and data often weren't the bottlenecks. The bottleneck was that scientists didn't know what to ask for from software, only how to express the problems they wanted to solve. I also found that most AI systems people were building didn't know how to meet scientists where they're at.

So now I'm trying to understand that gap better through a neolab I started with some friends. In the lab I've been building things from scratch. First an agent skill library and benchmarking harness for bioinformatics to see if agents can be made to reliably and reproducibly handle PhD+ levels of nuance. Also a JEPA based bioFM that tries to predict cell states and how they respond to perturbations to see if we can simulate biology instead of just measuring it. I also dabbled in a few other interests such as building consumer focused applications and vetting life science companies for VCs. Mainly I've been pursuing any opportunity that makes me think "hell yes" and avoiding the "mehs."
